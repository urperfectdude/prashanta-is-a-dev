This day looked normal at first, but it ended up teaching me a lot about money, communication, and emotional control.

I had gone to drop a friend at the airport. On the way, we stopped at an H&M store because he wanted to buy a hoodie. He also insisted on gifting me one. I initially refused because I do not usually wear expensive clothes, but he said it would be useful for my Kashmir trip and also a way to balance previous expenses. I accepted.

At the airport we hugged and said goodbye.

## The first discomfort: unspoken expectations with friends

After he left, I expected he might pay the cab fare. He did not, and I kept thinking whether I should message him for it.

The amount was not huge, but the discomfort was real.

I had already spent around 5k in different situations without tracking every rupee. Still, this time I felt like asking for my share. Then I stopped myself, thinking he was busy with international travel and this might feel petty.

So I stayed silent.

That silence taught me an important thing:

When money expectations are not discussed clearly, even close relationships can create internal friction.

## The second discomfort: fare confusion with the driver

On my way back, I asked a driver for a short drop near the metro. He first said "pay whatever, maybe 50 to 100." After reaching, he demanded 160. I negotiated to 120 and he agreed.

Then I requested a slightly different drop. He said metro road was blocked and suggested alternatives. I recalculated and thought reaching home directly might be simpler. He quoted 300, and I agreed.

After reaching, he suddenly demanded a total of 800.

That is when things escalated.

I argued because the numbers being discussed kept changing and the demand did not match what was agreed. He became loud, I got frustrated, and the conversation became public and unpleasant.

I finally paid more than I believed was fair, mainly to end the scene and avoid further disturbance near my place.

## What I learned from the whole incident

This was not just about one ride or one payment. It exposed a pattern in my behavior.

### 1) I avoid discomfort until it becomes bigger

Instead of discussing money early and clearly, I delay it to avoid awkwardness. Later, the stress becomes larger than the amount itself.

### 2) Verbal agreements are risky in ambiguous situations

If fare terms are unclear, confusion is almost guaranteed. In emotional moments, each person remembers numbers differently.

### 3) Peace has a cost, but so does unclear boundaries

Sometimes paying a little extra to end conflict is practical. But if this happens repeatedly, it trains people to exploit uncertainty.

### 4) Tone control is a real skill

When conflict gets loud, clear communication is harder. Remaining calm is not weakness. It is negotiation power.

## Practical rules I want to follow now

From now on, I want to use simple rules:

1. Confirm exact amount and destination before starting any ride.
2. Prefer app-based final fare whenever possible.
3. Clarify split expectations with friends before or during plans.
4. If a conflict starts escalating, close it fast and document details.
5. Never carry anger from one interaction into the next decision.

## Final reflection

The biggest lesson was not financial. It was emotional.

Money arguments are rarely about money alone. They are usually about communication gaps, assumptions, ego, and timing.

If I can improve clarity at the beginning, I can avoid frustration at the end.
