I wrote these notes while listening to an Osho discourse, and one idea stayed with me.

Most of what we call love is often mixed with fear, attachment, insecurity, expectation, or social convenience.

So the real question is not "Do I love someone?"
The real question is "What kind of love is this?"

The discourse pointed to three signs of true love, and I am trying to understand them in my own life.

## 1) No vested interest

If love is transactional, it is not love yet.

When I am with someone only for:

- money
- social status
- attention
- emotional dependency

then the relationship is driven by utility, not truth.

True love begins when I stop asking, "What can I get?"
and start living from, "How can I be present with honesty?"

This does not mean becoming blind or self-sacrificing in an unhealthy way.
It means the center of connection is not greed, fear, or image.

## 2) Ever-increasing bliss

Real love should deepen inner joy, not reduce it.

Attraction can be intense but short-lived.
Possession can feel powerful but eventually becomes heavy.
Control can look like care but slowly kills freedom.

True love, in contrast, creates expansion.
You become more alive, more creative, more peaceful.

The more awareness enters the relationship, the more joy grows.
Not excitement only, but a deeper kind of contentment.

## 3) It is not stopped even by death

This one sounds poetic, but it is profound.

If love is only body-level attachment, it ends with distance, conflict, or death.
If love is deeper than possession, it becomes a quality of being.

Then even when form changes, the gratitude, tenderness, and inner connection remain.

In that sense, true love is not just between two people.
It becomes a way of relating to existence itself.

## What this changed for me

This reflection made me question many of my own patterns:

- Do I confuse attachment with love?
- Do I expect too much validation from one person?
- Do I become possessive when insecure?
- Do I know how to love without controlling?

I do not have perfect answers yet.
But I can see that love matures when awareness matures.

## Final reflection

For me, true love is not drama, intensity, or emotional dependency.

It is:

- free from hidden transaction
- growing in inner joy
- rooted so deeply that even time cannot reduce its truth

I am still learning this.
But even understanding the direction changes how I see relationships.
