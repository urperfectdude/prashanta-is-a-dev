Today I finally had a talk with Omkar about veganism. My question was: even after having enough resources in terms of money, why don't you participate in veganism? The final answer boiled down to convenience, morals, perception and societal conditioning, since everything sums up to net zero. And never let someone else's personal morals replace your own, because that takes away your identity. But then:

1. Don't you care about climate and environmental causes, like carbon emissions?
2. Don't you have empathy when you see animals getting killed, abused and exploited?
3. Don't you feel bad when you exploit a mother mammal for her milk and leave her calves starving?

I didn't bring up health, because I knew health can be maintained easily with or without going vegan. That wasn't a point of conversation, as I was already convinced on that matter. For example, you can raise healthy chicken, healthy fish or organic plants.

1. This can be very subjective. You can say that animal farming, let's say chicken farming, creates a lot of carbon emissions and pollution. But then it can also be said: don't travel by vehicle, travel by walking. And when you look at any celebrity, they use private jets to travel when they could use other modes of transport. That brings us to the point that convenience is what makes us take the decision. Jain people wear masks to avoid killing germs, but they kill so many bacteria inside their bodies every day, and wearing a mask also kills germs by not letting them out. We could also argue that one shouldn't wear shoes or sandals and should walk barefoot, shouldn't build houses and should live under trees, and should wear tree leaves. So even if you don't go to the extreme, it's about convenience. People choose based on the morals or perception they have. For someone the bare minimum can be a scooter, for someone else it can be a BMW.

2. It's not that there is no empathy for animals. It's more about empathy in a larger sense. Plants also get hurt when you pluck fruits and veggies, and some scientists have suggested that plants do have emotions. It's just that they're not visible, or we haven't evolved to understand them, that's all. We have evolved to read facial expressions and emotions, and to know whether we should engage with a person based on whether they are angry or happy. Now assume an alien comes to earth tomorrow. Because it hasn't evolved to understand human expressions, it will be like, "OK, they have 2 eyes, 1 mouth, 2 ears, that's it." The way plants are for us now, humans will be for the aliens. People just don't care because there's no visible blood and they don't see the pain directly with their own eyes, based on evolution or the societal conditioning that has been done.

3. This is a win for both sides. If these animals weren't useful to humans, humans would not have bred them, and a lot of cattle would have been dinner for predators. They would always be living on high alert, getting scared by every movement and running to save their lives. But with humans, at least they are living a life and are not extinct. And when we talk about exploitation, like injecting animals with substances to boost productivity, the same thing goes for plants too. The original banana used to be small and full of seeds, but people modified its genetics to get the long seedless banana we have now. If the banana weren't useful, it would have stayed only in the forest, but because it's useful, it gets farmed, just like the animals. Another example is the wolf, the closest ancestor of the dog, which has a much shorter lifespan. Because dogs were able to make it into human society, they live longer than wolves. Same with goats. Same with chicken, pigs, buffalo, everything.
