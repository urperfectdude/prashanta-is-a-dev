---
title: "A Hard Night with a Friend"
date: "2025-03-26"
description: "A personal reflection on friendship, money boundaries, unspoken resentment, and the emotional mix of care, guilt, and comparison."
tags: ["journal", "mindset", "relationships", "finance"]
---

A close friend stayed with me in Delhi for a few days, and what looked like a normal hangout started becoming emotionally heavy for me.

We went out multiple times for food and drinks. There was no special occasion. I was trying to make sure he had a good last few days with me, especially because I had already said no to several expensive plans earlier. Somewhere inside, I think I was compensating for that guilt by saying yes too much later.

By the end of those outings, I had spent more than I had planned. That alone was not the real issue. The real issue was that I did not communicate my boundaries clearly, and then I started silently building frustration.

Another thing that bothered me was waste. I am usually careful while ordering and I do not like wasting food. But on those days we over-ordered more than once, and I could feel myself getting irritated. I stayed quiet in the moment, but mentally I was already counting every unnecessary expense.

At one point, my friend gave me practical advice: do not stay upset about not achieving enough, keep working until you make it. The advice was fair, but I still felt a strange mix of sadness and jealousy during the conversation, especially when he spoke about his assets and career progress.

That discomfort taught me something important. Sometimes we are not upset at the other person. We are upset at our own internal comparison, financial stress, and lack of boundaries.

There was also a conversation about AI and career direction. While listening, I felt conflicted. One part of me knew I had explored this space earlier. Another part of me felt left behind seeing others speak about it with fresh confidence. It was less about the topic and more about my own identity at that moment.

Looking back, this night was not really about drinks or bills. It was about emotional accounting.

I was trying to be generous without clarity.
I was trying to be available without limits.
I was trying to be happy for someone while quietly comparing paths.

Now I want to do this better.

If I care for someone, I should still define spending boundaries.
If something bothers me, I should communicate early.
If I feel comparison, I should turn it into action, not resentment.

Friendship should feel warm, not financially or emotionally performative.

This was a hard night, but it gave me one useful lesson: unspoken expectations always become hidden anger.
