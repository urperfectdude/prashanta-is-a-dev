date: "2026-08-30"
description: "A volunteering trip to Auroville meant for solitude and self improvement turned into two weeks pulled into two other people's slow-motion relationship, and what that revealed about how much I actually like having people around."
tags: ["travel", "relationships", "journal", "people"]
---

When I came to Auroville for volunteering, I had a very different plan in my head. I wanted to use this time almost like a self retreat where I could work on my apps, learn some new things, start creating content, follow a proper routine and spend some time with myself because I don't really socialise that much and I thought this would be a good opportunity to focus on myself without too many distractions.

During the first week I met Piyush. He is quite well spoken, smart and has done some interesting things in the past, especially around ads and that kind of work, so initially I found him interesting to talk to. But there was one thing which immediately made me keep some distance from him. In our first week itself he told me that he wanted to stay away from intoxications because he used to do a lot of it when he was in Bangalore, but then I started seeing him smoking weed and cigarettes repeatedly throughout the day. I don't really like being around smoke because it makes me uncomfortable, so my immediate thought was that this person doesn't really have much control over himself and I didn't want to get too close to that kind of crowd.

So I started staying away from him and people who were mostly into smoking and drinking.

Then in the second week Rishika came.

She was very different from what I had seen around there. She is extremely empathetic, especially towards animals, she genuinely cares about people and somehow understands what the other person is feeling quite well. She has had her own problems with breakups, relationships and figuring out what she wants to do with her life, but even with all that she never really made other people feel like they had to carry her problems. At the same time she can also be very impulsive and do some really crazy things without thinking too much, but somehow she always stays very chill about everything.

She had some situationship going on before coming here and then on the first day she was interested in one guy to a large extent, and when that guy left, the next day she started getting interested in Piyush.

And somehow, because both of them were very extroverted and I was spending time around them, I also started liking being around Rishika. What was different this time was that I had actually started lowering my own filters. Normally I keep a certain distance from people because I don't want too many people entering my personal space and disturbing my peace or routine, but with these two I started allowing them into my zone.

I started having deeper conversations with both of them and talking about things which I usually don't tell people very quickly. Piyush especially seemed quite interested in knowing about me and my life. He would keep asking about my work, my routine, what I had done before, how I manage certain things and many other personal things. Initially I kept avoiding most of it but eventually I told him a lot.

Later I understood that one reason he was so interested in me was probably because he saw my routine and discipline as something he wanted for himself. He had lost around 20 lakhs through trading and didn't really have a job at that point, so he was also trying to figure out what he wanted to do with his life.

I think this also became one of the reasons I stayed interested in him. I liked the fact that he looked at something in my life and thought that maybe he could learn from it. There was a small part of me which liked the idea of teaching him something or helping him get his life a little more organised. I don't think I consciously thought about it like this at that time, but looking back, I think it was definitely one of the reasons I kept the connection.

Then Rishika had to leave for Pondicherry because of some plan, and surprisingly I felt quite bad about it. Piyush was very attached to her by then and he was also looking quite depressed and listening to sad songs and all that. Since I was spending a lot of time with him, somehow I also started feeling sad.

Then that same night Rishika came back because her bookings were apparently for the wrong dates and were actually for the next month. I was genuinely very happy to see her again and we hugged and everything, and after that we started doing these random midnight bike rides and hanging out together.

This is where I think I started losing myself a little bit.

There were things I normally say no to which I started saying yes to. I started standing around them while they were smoking even though I don't like smoke. I started skipping my own work and routine just to spend more time with them. I wasted a couple of days where I wasn't really doing the things I had actually come to Auroville to do.

At that time I didn't think too much about it because honestly I was enjoying it. I was socialising, meeting people, going on random rides and having conversations late at night, which is not something I normally do. So there was also a good side to it. I realised that maybe I actually do enjoy having people around me more than I thought, I just usually don't let people get close enough.

But then I started noticing what was actually happening between Rishika and Piyush.

They were clearly falling for each other, even though neither of them was admitting it. They were spending most of their time together and there were enough obvious signs that I could understand what was going on. At one point I even saw hickeys on both of them, so there wasn't really much left to understand.

And I remember thinking, why the hell am I doing this?

I came here to work on myself, build things, learn new technology and start creating content. Instead I had somehow become part of the emotional world of two people who were falling in love.

I was even giving both of them advice about their relationship.

For Piyush, I told him that if he genuinely cared about Rishika, he shouldn't take her concerns about his financial situation as proof that her feelings were fake. They had only known each other for a few weeks and it was completely normal for her to think about whether someone is financially stable enough for a long term relationship. I told him that instead of trying to prove himself through words, he should focus on his work, his goals, his responsibility and the actual steps he was taking to improve his situation. He didn't have to become successful immediately, but he should show that he was moving somewhere.

For Rishika, I told her that wanting a partner who is financially responsible and capable of building a stable future doesn't make her materialistic. At the same time, she had only known him for a few weeks and it was too early to decide what his entire future would look like based only on his current situation. I told her to look at his character, ambition, work ethic and whether he actually wants to improve his life, because there is a difference between someone who is temporarily struggling and someone who simply doesn't want to take responsibility.

Looking back, it is quite funny that I came here to work on myself and ended up giving relationship advice to two people who were basically falling in love in front of me.

Then Rishika left again.

I thought this time I would simply go back to my own routine and focus on what I had planned to do. The first one or two days were actually a little difficult because I had become used to the constant social interaction, but eventually I got back into my routine and started doing my own things again.

Then after around four days she came back again for another two or three days.

I was surprised and obviously happy to see her, but this time something felt different inside me. The attachment was much lower compared to the previous time.

I think I understand why now.

Earlier I was simply enjoying the three of us being together. I had not really thought about the bigger dynamic. But after seeing how strongly Rishika and Piyush were getting attached to each other, I started feeling like I was becoming the third person in their relationship.

It was no longer really the same thing.

Earlier it felt like three people becoming friends.

Now it felt more like two people were becoming a couple and I was somewhere around them.

And I think that naturally made me less interested in being around them all the time.

I don't think it is because I am angry at either of them or because I don't like them anymore. I still think both of them are interesting people and I still care about them. But I don't really want to become that person who is always around a couple, listening to their relationship problems, joining all their plans and slowly becoming part of their relationship without actually being part of it.

I also think the reason I still feel connected to Piyush is slightly different.

I think I liked the fact that he looked at me as someone who had some things figured out. He was interested in my discipline and routine and maybe wanted to learn from me. I liked being able to tell him things and maybe help him think differently about his life.

But I also realised that there is a difference between teaching someone something and taking responsibility for them.

I can tell him what worked for me. I can give him advice. I can tell him how I think about discipline and work and maybe even help him if he asks. But ultimately he has to decide what he wants to do with his life. I don't need to stay constantly connected to him just because I feel like I can teach him something.

The bigger thing I learned from all of this is probably about myself.

I always thought my solution was to keep strong filters around people and not let too many people into my life because that protects my routine and peace. But this experience showed me that I actually do enjoy people. I enjoy random conversations, spontaneous plans, midnight rides and having people around me. I just don't want all of that to come at the cost of my own life.

I don't want to become so strict that I don't experience anything, but I also don't want to become so involved with people that I forget why I started something in the first place.

Maybe I don't need to completely close my guards or completely open them.

Maybe I just need to learn how to let people into my life without giving them control over it.

I came to Auroville thinking this would mostly be a period of solitude and self improvement, but instead I ended up meeting two people who completely changed the experience for me for a couple of weeks.

I lost some discipline, wasted some time, got emotionally involved, discovered that I actually enjoy socialising and also realised how easily I can get pulled into someone else's world when I genuinely like their company.

Maybe that itself was worth learning.

The goal now is not to stop caring about people.

It is just to make sure that while being around them, I don't forget myself.
