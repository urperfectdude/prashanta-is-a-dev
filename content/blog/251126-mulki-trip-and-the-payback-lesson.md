This story started as pure chaos and ended as a clarity moment.

Back in August, I was stuck late at the office when Omkar called. We decided to catch up over dinner at a bar-restaurant he likes for pizza. I was genuinely happy to see him, though I noticed he had lost a lot of weight. The first half hour was awkward because he was glued to his phone, chatting with someone else, and I had to jokingly tell him to focus on dinner.

While ordering, I did my usual cost-saving routine with discounts and offers. The funny part was that I ended up drinking both free beers myself because he would not drink. Then came a moment that perfectly reflects his personality. I reached for a packaged water bottle and he immediately stopped me, saying we would lose money on it. I checked the menu, opened it anyway, and laughed at how intense he was about even small spend decisions.

Somewhere between pizza and that water-bottle debate, we started discussing travel. I rejected Hampi since I had gone recently, and we landed on Mulki because I wanted to try surfing. Within about 45 minutes, the whole plan was locked. Bus in one hour.

I rushed back to pack. He suggested going almost bag-less, just with a charger, which was too wild even for me. I still packed very light, forgot basic things, and we rolled with the mess. The trip energy was impulsive, funny, and completely unplanned.

On the way, he shared financial details openly, including a net worth far above mine. I shared my reality too, supporting family responsibilities while trying to grow. The contrast was large, but the conversation was honest. That bus ride turned into hours of deep discussion about life, relationships, and personal baggage.

At that point I thought the biggest takeaway from Mulki would be spontaneity.
I was wrong.

The real lesson came later.

Months after the trip, I had to decide whether to pay Omkar ₹8,600 for my share of expenses, even though he had told me I could keep it. A part of me felt I was losing a chance to save money. But then I remembered how he had repeatedly followed up on a smaller unrelated amount from another person.

That made something clear.

He does not "forget money." He is intentional with it.
He can be generous, but he also tracks accountability.

Once I understood that, my decision became easy. I paid him back.

For me, that payment was not about transaction math. It was about relationship hygiene. I did not want even one hidden layer of doubt where he might wonder if I was taking advantage of his generosity because he could afford it.

By paying, I bought clarity.
No awkwardness.
No silent debt.
No emotional accounting in future conversations.

I gave up a one-time saving and got long-term peace of mind.
That trade feels right every time.
