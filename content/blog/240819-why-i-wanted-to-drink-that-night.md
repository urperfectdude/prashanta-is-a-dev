That day started with someone else's disappointment.

A friend had missed his judicial interview selection by just two marks. The atmosphere around us was heavy. I was half asleep during the conversation, but when I woke up, I casually said, "Should we go to a bar?" Everyone laughed, and he said he does not drink. Another friend asked me if I really wanted to drink, and I said no because I had exams coming.

At that point, I genuinely did not want alcohol.

But later, during another conversation, someone asked why I never go to bars. That small comment triggered something in me. Suddenly I decided to go. The decision was not about taste or relaxation. It was more about image, curiosity, and a hidden desire to feel socially confident around strangers, especially women.

## What happened at the bar

When I reached there, I still did not feel like drinking.

While ordering, I asked for a small quantity, around 30 ml, just to try. The staff pushed for a larger serving and convinced me to buy 180 ml. I agreed even though I was unsure.

Then I tasted white rum and instantly knew I did not like it. I asked if it could be changed, but they refused.

In the same setting, I noticed two women and felt an urge to impress them. I even asked the waiter to send a beer to one of them and point toward me if she asked who sent it. The waiter refused, saying the manager would not allow it. I did not push further. I tried to get attention for a moment, saw no interest, and stopped.

## What I understood after this

The most important insight is this:

I did not go there from desire. I went there from emotional confusion.

I was carrying a mix of things:

- second-hand sadness from the people around me
- social pressure to appear outgoing
- insecurity about being left out
- fantasy of instant validation

Alcohol was only a surface action. The real story was internal.

## Mistakes I made

When I replay the night, I can see my mistakes clearly:

- I made a decision based on social provocation, not intention
- I ignored my own first instinct, which was "I do not want this"
- I allowed persuasion to overrule my boundary while ordering
- I entered social interactions from neediness, not respect and confidence

Nothing extreme happened, but the pattern is worth catching early.

## Better rules for myself

I am writing this so I remember practical rules next time:

1. If my first instinct says no, I should trust it.
2. I should not make mood-based decisions when I feel emotionally unsettled.
3. I should decide quantity and budget before entering places like bars.
4. I should never approach people for validation, only for genuine conversation.
5. I should leave a place early if my intention is unclear.

## Final reflection

That night was not about alcohol. It was about identity.

I learned that confidence is not proving I can enter a bar or get attention. Confidence is being able to walk away when something does not align with who I want to become.

This note is a reminder to choose clarity over impulse.
