A friend went through my resume on a call. They didn't start with the formatting or the bullet points. They started with the top of the page.

"You've written that you're a product focused person. You're applying for product roles. And your top three roles are designer, software engineer and consultant."

Then the part that stuck with me: "I'm the guy looking at your resume. How would I hire you for a PM role? This guy has done everything."

They weren't being harsh about it. They said they used to make the same mistake. You put everything on the page because every line feels like proof of something. But on a resume, more doesn't add up. It thins out. Someone skimming for a product manager sees an engineer, then a designer, then a consultant, and stops looking for the product person hiding somewhere in between.

My honest reply was, but those are my actual titles. What am I supposed to do?

They didn't have a clean answer either. They said they tell people not to put HTML and CSS on their resumes, and people say that's what they have. "And you're right too. I can't blame you."

The part I keep coming back to is the comparison. My resume isn't sitting alone on someone's desk. It's in a pile next to people whose last three titles say Product Manager, Product Manager, Senior Product Manager. In isolation, mine is fine. Next to theirs, it reads weaker. My friend put it plainly: "That makes your resume weak. Not because you are a weak person."

It also lands uncomfortably close to something I already wrote about myself. Design, then engineering, then consulting is the same switching pattern I described in [I Quit Too Early, Every Time](/blog/260828-i-quit-too-early-every-time). The resume isn't misrepresenting me. It's representing me pretty accurately, and that's the hard part.

A title is what a company called me. It doesn't say what I spent my days deciding. If there's real product work inside those roles, the resume has to show it in the first few seconds, through what I chose to build, what I cut, and what changed because of it. The title line won't do that for me.

And if there isn't enough product work in there to show, that's an answer too. A more useful one than a better template.

So I'm going through it one line at a time with a single question: would someone hiring a PM care about this? If not, it goes, even if it was work I was proud of.
