I think one of the most underrated problems in life is having too many genuine interests.

I do not mean distraction.
I do not mean shiny object syndrome.
I mean real curiosity that keeps pulling you in many directions at once.

I have worked in tech professionally, explored media and hardware for years, and now I am diving deeper into core AI engineering.

At the same time, I am deeply fascinated by biology, physiology, evolution, nutrition, and medicine.

Then there is another side of me that loves sports, music production, strength training, discipline, and physical competence.

And yes, a part of me still wants to get a pilot license one day, just to experience flying firsthand.

Somehow, I am equally obsessed with space as well.

## The hidden cost of multi-passion curiosity

For a long time, this created constant FOMO in my mind:

- What if I had chosen medicine?
- What if I had continued media seriously?
- What if I had gone into aerospace?

These were not random thoughts. They were emotionally heavy because each path felt meaningful in its own way.

When you care deeply about many fields, every decision feels like a permanent loss.

## What changed for me

Eventually, I realized I was asking the wrong question.

I was not searching for the perfect field.

I was searching for a field that gives me freedom to explore multiple versions of myself over time.

That shift changed everything.

## Why tech became my base layer

For me, tech became the sweet spot.

It gives me:

- autonomy to create things independently
- creative range from code to design to product
- flexibility to move across domains
- leverage to build fast and test ideas in the real world

In one phase, I can focus on app development.
In another, I can move toward media systems.
Now I can dive into AI engineering.
Later I can build at the intersection of health, performance, education, or domains that are not even clearly defined yet.

Tech does not force me to become one fixed identity.
It gives me a platform to evolve.

## A different way to define focus

I used to think focus means picking one box and staying there forever.

Now I see focus differently.

Focus can also mean choosing one powerful base, then compounding from it while exploring adjacent frontiers with intent.

That is not confusion.
That is structured curiosity.

## Final reflection

If you are someone with many deep interests, you are not broken.

Your challenge is not lack of direction.
Your challenge is designing a life architecture that can hold your curiosity without fragmenting your progress.

For me, tech is that architecture.

Not because it is trendy.
Because it gives me the freedom to keep becoming.
