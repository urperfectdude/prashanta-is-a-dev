---
title: "Thinking About Ego"
date: "2025-11-14"
description: "A reflection on inner and outer ego, and how the same want feels different once the reason behind it moves outside of us."
tags: ["mindset", "people", "health", "finance"]
---

I have been thinking about ego lately, and I am not fully sure I am using the word correctly. I don't mean the technical psychological definition. I mean the part of us that keeps reaching for something and then attaches meaning to it: what we have, what we achieve, how we are seen, where we stand next to other people.

I think this can move in two directions.

The first I call the inner ego. The reference point is my own experience. I want more money because I want freedom or security. I want to be healthier because I want to feel better in my body.

The second is the outer ego, where the reference point slowly moves outside. I still want more money, but now part of the reason is being seen as successful. I still want to be fit, but now I need other people to find me attractive.

The thing I want is exactly the same. The reason changed.

I don't think these are established psychological categories, but they overlap with real research. Tim Kasser and Richard Ryan separated extrinsic aspirations, like financial success and fame, from intrinsic ones, like personal growth and close relationships. People who put the extrinsic ones at the centre tended to report lower well-being. There is also work on social comparison, on zero-sum beliefs, on contingent self-esteem (worth that depends on hitting certain outcomes) and on self-transcendence.

Two people can both exercise every morning. One enjoys feeling energetic. The other cannot stand being judged for how they look. From the outside you can't tell them apart. Same behaviour, completely different inner experience.

This is where I started thinking about a zero point of ego. I mean a point where I can want something without it becoming a measure of my worth.

If I want to be healthier, I can just want to be healthier. Someone else being healthier doesn't take anything from mine. Some things really are competitive, one job or one prize, but most of life isn't.

The mind still turns it into a competition. I don't want to earn enough, I want to earn more than someone else.

What surprised me is that I don't need anyone to tell me what to want anymore. I already know what a successful person is supposed to look like. I can be completely alone and still check how an achievement would look to other people. I have internalised the audience.

That might explain why achievement never feels like enough. If a goal only gave me the thing, I would eventually be satisfied. But if it is also supposed to prove my worth, there is never enough evidence. I reach a position I once dreamed about, and it becomes normal. The goalposts move because I am not chasing the object anymore. I am chasing what it is supposed to prove about me.

The inner ego asks, "What would make my life better?" The outer ego asks, "What would make me look better?" Both can lead to the same action.

I don't think the answer is wanting nothing. Maybe what changes is my relationship with the want. And maybe at some point the question shifts again: if I become wealthy, what can I do with that wealth? If I learn a lot, what can I do with that knowledge? I am still growing, but the self doesn't have to be the final destination.

These aren't fixed stages. I can want something for myself in the morning and spend the afternoon comparing myself with someone else. The point is catching the moment the motivation changes. A few questions help:

If nobody knew I had it, would I still want it?

If nobody praised me for it, would it still matter?

If someone else had more, would I still be happy with mine?

They won't remove ego. They might make it a little easier to see.

Maybe the problem isn't that we have a self. Maybe it starts when we stop noticing what that self is chasing and why. The closest thing to a zero point might be pausing there for a moment and seeing the desire before turning it into a story about who we are.
