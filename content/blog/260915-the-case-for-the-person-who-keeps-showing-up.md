---
title: "The Case for the Person Who Keeps Showing Up"
date: "2026-09-15"
description: "A friend believes attraction is either there from the start or it never will be, so you shouldn't invest in anyone who doesn't show interest early. Two people in my own life only got close to me because they kept trying."
tags: ["relationships", "people", "journal"]
---

A friend and I got into a long argument about attraction.

Their view goes like this. A lot of men approach relationships the way they approach work. Be useful, be supportive, be the nice friend, and eventually it turns into something. My friend thinks it doesn't work that way. Genuine attraction isn't negotiated. When someone is into you, you don't have to guess. If you catch yourself building reasons why they'll come around ("they just haven't seen enough of me yet"), you already have your answer.

So their rule is to filter first. Don't pour effort into someone who shows no interest at the start.

They had a second worry too. The slow path, where you talk to someone every day until you become a habit in their life, does sometimes work. But they've seen it go badly. People spend years on someone who was never interested. And the person who worked that hard to get in often holds on too tightly once they're there.

I agree with a lot of that. Nobody wants to spend years on someone who was never going to feel the same way.

But I pushed back on the rule itself. If everyone waits for the other person to show interest first, who starts anything?

I had two examples from my own life.

There was a guy who came up to talk to me every day. I wasn't talking to anyone much at the time. That's just my natural setting. I gave him almost nothing back. He kept coming anyway. Slowly we started actually talking, and now he's a really good friend. If he had gone by the signals I was giving, it never would have happened.

The second was a girl who kept inviting me to events. I kept not going. She kept inviting me. Eventually I went, and now we're connected. If she had read my silence as a no and stopped, there would be nothing there.

My friend's answer was that neither of those counts. Their rule was only ever about one specific situation, a man romantically pursuing a woman, and friendships don't follow the same logic. I'm not convinced the line is that clean. But I also don't have a romantic example to put against it, so I'll leave that part open.

What I do know is that low interest at the start isn't always a no. Sometimes it's just someone who's slow to open up. I'm that person. The first signals I give almost anyone are flat. Under my friend's rule, most people would have walked away from me, and a couple of the people I value most would never have become part of my life.

Where my friend is right is about the difference between two kinds of persistence. One ignores a clear no and tries to wear someone down. The other just keeps a door open. The guy who kept talking to me never pressured me into anything. He was friendly and he was around, and he left the rest up to me.

Maybe the rule isn't "only invest in people who already like you." Maybe it's "don't keep investing in someone who has told you no." Those sound close. They lead to very different lives.
