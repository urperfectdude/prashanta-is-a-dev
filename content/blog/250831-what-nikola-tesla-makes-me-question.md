Whenever I think about Nikola Tesla, I feel both inspired and uncomfortable.

He worked relentlessly to change the world, yet he died poor. He was driven more by vision than by personal wealth. His belief seemed clear: if technology saves human effort and time, people should use that extra capacity to create greater value for humanity.

That ideal is beautiful, but it also creates a hard question for me.

Are we actually using the leverage we inherited from people like him in meaningful ways?

Today we have access to electricity, communication, automation, and tools that earlier generations could not even imagine. We can build faster, learn faster, and distribute ideas globally in minutes. But the same leverage can also be wasted in distraction, shallow consumption, and endless reaction loops.

When I reflect on Tesla's life, I do not only think about invention. I think about intention.

He did not optimize for comfort.
He optimized for contribution.

He did not ask first, "How much will I get?"
He asked, "How much can this improve human life?"

That contrast challenges me personally.

Am I using modern tools to create real value, or just to stay busy?
Am I compounding capability, or just compounding noise?
Am I building things that help people, or only chasing output that looks impressive?

For me, this reflection is less about hero worship and more about responsibility.

If we benefit from the sacrifices of people who built foundational systems, then the minimum respect we can give them is not admiration. It is disciplined use of that leverage.

This is the standard I want to hold:

Use technology with intention.
Create more than you consume.
Let convenience increase contribution, not laziness.

That is the kind of life Tesla's story pushes me to think about.
