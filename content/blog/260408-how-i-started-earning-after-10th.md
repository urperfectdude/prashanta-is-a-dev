I started paying for my own expenses and education fees right after 10th grade.

Everything began during the COVID lockdown.

While most people around me were passing time at home, I started working at a local computer shop as a computer technician.

It was hands-on work from day one:

- fixing desktop and laptop issues
- installing Windows
- troubleshooting random hardware and software problems
- learning by doing, every single day

At that time, I was not thinking about career strategy. I was just trying to stay useful and independent.

But that one small start created momentum.

Over time, the journey expanded into:

- a UI design internship with an overseas company
- freelance video editing work
- no-code web and app development projects

None of this came from a perfect plan.

I was not waiting for ideal conditions, the best course, or the "right time." I just kept stacking one skill over another while moving with whatever opportunities I had.

Looking back, that compounding changed everything.

Yes, money mattered. It helped me handle my own responsibilities early.

But the biggest thing I earned was not money.

It was confidence.

The confidence that no matter what happens, I can figure things out.

That belief came from real work, not motivation videos.

When you solve enough small problems with your own hands, you stop being scared of big problems. You know you can adapt, learn, and rebuild.

And for me, that has been the most valuable return from this whole journey.
