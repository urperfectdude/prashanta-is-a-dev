Some of the most unexpected life reflections come from short bike rides with strangers.

On this day, I took two Rapido rides in Delhi, and both conversations stayed with me for very different reasons.

The first ride was from Vasant Vihar to JNU. The rider was around 23, and while riding he started sharing his relationship story. He had been with his girlfriend since school, almost from class 6, so around nine years together. But even after all this time, they were still struggling to convince their families for marriage.

Listening to him made me think about how long relationships are not always strong relationships. Sometimes people stay together for years but still do not build clarity, courage, or alignment for difficult life decisions. I kept wondering whether he had really tried enough, whether he lacked confidence, or whether both of them were emotionally tired by family pressure. It felt less like a love failure and more like a negotiation failure with reality.

The second ride was from JNU to Bharat Mandapam. This rider was 21, doing BCom, and driving part-time to earn pocket money. His name was Danish, a Delhi-born Muslim guy. What surprised me was that he had never visited India Gate properly, even after living in Delhi all his life. When I asked what he liked in the city, his answers were vague and avoidant. Even for basic nearby landmarks, he started guessing rather than saying he did not know.

Then I played along and said I was new to Delhi. His tone changed instantly and he started giving me city-safety advice with full confidence, warning me about thieves and scams. That shift was interesting. People often speak with certainty not because they know, but because they want to look like they know.

Both rides showed me different sides of the same human pattern. In one case, someone could not confidently stand for what mattered most to him. In the other, someone tried to project confidence even where knowledge was weak.

By the end of the day, I felt that adulthood is mostly about this balance, being honest about what you do not know, and being courageous about what you truly value. Most people either fake certainty or avoid commitment. Very few do both honesty and courage together.

Delhi gave me traffic, noise, and random routes that day, but these two conversations gave me a sharper lens on people and on myself.
