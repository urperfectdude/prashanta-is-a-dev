I have been thinking about this for the last few days and I don't really know how to put it. I don't want to say that Pondicherry is unsafe for girls because I don't think I have enough experience here to make such a big statement. But at the same time, some of the things I have seen recently have honestly made me feel very uncomfortable.

It started with one incident, then another one happened, then I heard about another girl facing something similar, and after some point I was like, why am I hearing so many of these stories in such a short time?

The first incident was with a girl who was travelling solo. She was coming from Bangalore to Pondicherry and then to Auroville for the first time. She reached Pondicherry around 4 in the morning and booked a Rapido to come here.

Before the actual Rapido guy arrived, another guy on a bike came and asked her about the ride. The actual ride was for around ₹100, but this guy said he would take her for ₹150.

Because it was 4 in the morning and she was alone, she agreed.

She sat on his bike and they started going towards Auroville.

After around one or two kilometres, she got a call from the actual Rapido driver saying, "Hi ma'am, I am at the location. Where are you?"

That's when she realised that she was on the bike of a completely different person.

She got really scared and started asking the guy to stop. She wanted to get down, but the guy wasn't stopping. Instead, he told her that he was the Rapido driver's friend and that he would talk to him.

The girl was from Kerala and she understood a little Tamil, so she could understand enough of the conversation to realise that something was not right.

Thankfully, she managed to share her location with the actual Rapido driver and he came and found her.

Later she was telling us that she was also regretting not asking for the OTP before getting onto the bike. And honestly, I don't even blame her for that. It was 4 in the morning, she had just arrived in a new place and someone was there to take her. In that moment, you don't necessarily stop and think about every safety check.

But that incident itself was already quite scary.

Then there was another incident which I was much more involved in.

This one happened during Onam celebrations at the hostel. The same guy who had been staying with us for a few days showed up three hours late, right around dinner time, without a ticket. He lied to the people distributing food anyway and got himself a plate.

Later, when we were all leaving to head back to the hostel, we didn't have enough vehicles for everyone. One guy from our own group suggested that one of the girls go with this stranger since he had a bike. She agreed and went with him.

Once the rest of us reached the hostel, I noticed she still wasn't back. I called her.

She said the guy wanted ice cream, so they had stopped somewhere, and she thought it was fine.

About thirty minutes later I called again. This time her voice was panicked. She texted me not to disconnect the call until she reached the hostel and shared her live location. I could hear her telling him to turn back, that this wasn't the right route, and him brushing her off, telling her not to worry.

He kept trying to film her on his phone to post on social media. He also kept asking if she wanted to get into dancing or acting, saying he had a lot of connections and money and could help her. She told him clearly that she wasn't looking for any of that, she just wanted to go back.

Her location showed them going back and forth on some long route through the forest. Watching that on the map while staying on the call with her was one of the worst ten minutes of that whole week. I just tried to stay calm and wait for her.

They eventually made it back. She ran straight into her dorm, visibly shaken, and I sat with her for a while trying to calm her down.

That same night we were all playing Mafia on the terrace and sharing phones to follow each other on Instagram. At some point he got hold of her phone, accepted his own follow request from her account, and sent himself a message from it. I told him to just pass the phone back. She blocked him right after.

The next day he tried something similar with another girl, from Kerala this time. She came and told me about it because she felt comfortable enough to. I told her to stay away from him. Since he now had her number, he started texting her things like "hello princess." She blocked him too.

I complained to the property manager about all of this and asked if something could actually be done, whether he could be removed or at least kept from coming back. He told me he had already emailed the team about it. Two weeks later I found out he never had.

And even after all that, on another day, he tried the same act on a new girl who had just arrived, telling her he would show her around and take her to all the good places, that he had a lot of contacts.

Because I don't think it is enough to just tell the girls to be careful.

There was also another incident I heard about where a cab driver was asking girls about their hostel and room details. He was also asking whether drivers would be allowed inside and things like that.

I don't really know what the intention was, but hearing something like this after everything else just felt very strange.

Around the same time, I heard about a girl from Kerala who had come to Pondicherry to celebrate her birthday and wanted to spend the night at Rock Beach, but left earlier than planned because a lot of the men there made her uncomfortable.

She also mentioned that the roads back to the Auroville hostel have no street lights at all, and travelling that stretch alone at night is genuinely scary for a girl by herself.

And then there was one more incident which honestly shocked me because it happened inside the GoStops property itself.

A local guy came inside the property saying he wanted water. While he was there, he walked up to a girl and casually asked her what her "rate" was, along with a few other insulting things.

The girl got so angry that she told him to get lost. The property caretakers stepped in and asked him to leave.

What surprised me afterwards was that a few solo travellers staying there who already knew the girl didn't step in at all while this was happening. Only one guy actually helped her once things had gone bad. I wasn't even around for any of it, I was at the gym when it happened.

But the fact that a random person could enter the property and talk to a girl like that was disturbing to me.

Maybe this is something that happens in every city and I am just noticing it more because I am here right now. Maybe because I am volunteering and meeting so many travellers, I am hearing these stories directly from the girls instead of just hearing about them somewhere online.

I don't know.

I also don't want to compare Pondicherry with Mumbai or Delhi and say those cities are safe and this one isn't. Bad things happen everywhere. But I have lived in and travelled around places like Mumbai and Delhi and I don't remember hearing this many creepy incidents involving girls within such a short period.

And I think that is what has been bothering me.

You can tell someone to check the OTP, share their location, not get onto a random bike and not tell strangers where they are staying. Of course all of that is important. But you can't really go through every normal interaction thinking that the person in front of you might have some bad intention.

The girl who got onto the wrong bike probably didn't think that the person coming to pick her up could be someone else. The other girl also didn't think that someone who was staying in the same hostel and offering to drop her back would suddenly take her somewhere else.

You can't always know.

And I think that is what makes these things scary.

I don't know if Pondicherry is actually unsafe for girls. I don't think it would be fair for me to make that conclusion from these incidents alone.

But I also can't ignore what I have actually seen and heard.

After all this, I have started paying much more attention to these things. I don't suddenly think everyone around me is dangerous. I have just realised how quickly a normal situation can turn uncomfortable once someone decides to cross a boundary.

And honestly, I just wish people could travel, meet new people and stay in hostels without having to think so much about these things.
